Fit every exam into the fewest slots.

Subjects become dots. Two subjects that share a student get a line between them. Coloring the dots so that no line joins two matching colors gives a clash-free timetable, and the fewest colors is the fewest slots. Backtracking finds that number.

    How the search thinks

    Four moves are enough to understand the whole algorithm.

    1
    Guess a slot count

    Start with k = 1 slot. If that fails, try 2, then 3. The first k that works is the minimum.

    2
    Place one subject

    Take the busiest subject first. Give it the lowest slot that none of its neighbours already uses.

    3
    Check for clashes

    A neighbour in the same slot means a shared student, so that slot is rejected and the next one is tried.

    4
    Backtrack when stuck

    If a subject has no valid slot, undo the previous choice and try something else. If everything fails, move to k + 1.

    Speed-ups

    Busiest subjects first, and no new slot is opened until it is needed, so mirror-image colorings are never repeated.

    Cost

    Worst case grows like kⁿ. Graph coloring is NP-hard, so large inputs use heuristics such as DSATUR. A school’s subject list is small enough for exact search.

    Same idea elsewhere

    Mobile tower frequencies, compiler register allocation, map coloring, and Sudoku all reduce to coloring a graph.
